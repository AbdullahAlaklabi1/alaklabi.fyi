My research focuses on lightweight multimedia security for images and video. I study encryption architectures that combine chaotic systems, substitution–permutation networks, transform-domain processing, and steganography to improve confidentiality while keeping computation practical.

A central goal is to evaluate not only whether the encrypted output appears random, but also whether the system is sensitive to keys and plaintext changes, resistant to statistical analysis, and efficient across different multimedia sizes.

01Z-Crypt — Image Encryption with CLM + SPN + CZT ▶PUBLISHED · 2024
An image-encryption architecture combining chaotic logistic-map shuffling, substitution–permutation processing, XOR-based diffusion/whitening, and the Chirp Z-Transform. The design is evaluated using statistical, differential, information-theoretic, and randomness-oriented metrics.

02Hybrid Encryption + Steganography — SPN + CLM + LSB ▶PUBLISHED · 2026
A hybrid approach that combines image encryption with LSB-based data hiding. Chaotic permutation and SPN processing protect the payload while the embedding stage hides encrypted information inside cover images, with emphasis on low execution time and visual imperceptibility.
03Lightweight Video Encryption — CLM + SPN ▶MANUSCRIPT
A frame-oriented video-encryption method using SHA-256-derived values, chaotic row/column shuffling, SPN substitution/permutation, and XOR operations. The work evaluates security behavior and practical runtime across multiple video sequences.
